Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Criminal cases raise urgent questions. Here are the ones most clients ask first. For anything else, call directly.

Is what I tell Matthew confidential?
Yes. Everything you share with Matthew is protected by attorney-client privilege, one of the strongest legal protections that exists. This means he cannot and will not disclose anything you tell him to police, prosecutors, or anyone else. This privilege applies from your very first conversation, including the free consultation. You can speak freely and honestly so he can give you the best possible advice.
Does the free consultation stay confidential too?
Yes. Attorney-client privilege attaches from the moment you first consult with an attorney, even before you formally retain them. What you tell Matthew during the free consultation cannot be used against you and cannot be shared with anyone. You do not need to hold back out of fear that the information will go further.
Should I talk to police before hiring an attorney?
No. You have the right to remain silent, and you should use it. Anything you say to police can and will be used against you in court. Politely decline to answer questions and ask to speak with an attorney immediately. This is true even if you are innocent. Misstatements made under pressure can be used to damage your case later.
How much does a criminal defense attorney cost?
Fees vary based on the complexity of the charges, the stage of the case, and whether the matter goes to trial. Matthew offers flexible payment arrangements and will be upfront about fees during your free consultation. The cost of not having strong representation is almost always higher.
What happens at a preliminary hearing?
A preliminary hearing is one of the most important stages in a Pennsylvania criminal case. The judge decides whether the Commonwealth has enough evidence to proceed. Matthew has had countless cases dismissed or significantly reduced at this stage. It is an opportunity that should not be wasted with unprepared representation.
What is a motion to suppress?
A motion to suppress challenges whether evidence against you was obtained legally. If police violated your constitutional rights during a search or arrest, a judge may exclude that evidence from trial. In many cases, a successful suppression motion means the charges are dropped entirely.
Can Matthew help if I have already been charged?
Yes. Matthew handles cases at every stage, from the moment of arrest through trial and beyond. The earlier you get representation the better, but it is never too late to call.
What if I can't afford an attorney?
Matthew understands that legal fees are a real concern, especially when you are already under enormous stress. He offers flexible payment arrangements and will work with you to find a structure that fits your situation. Call for a free consultation and have an honest conversation about costs. There is no pressure and no obligation.
How quickly can Matthew take my case?
Matthew understands that criminal cases are urgent. He responds to inquiries quickly and can often schedule a same-day or next-day consultation. If you or a loved one is in custody, call directly and he will prioritize getting you the information you need right away.
Will Matthew personally handle my case?
Yes. Boyd Law LLC is a dedicated criminal defense practice and Matthew personally handles every case. You will not be handed off to a junior associate or lost in a large firm. When you hire Matthew, you get Matthew: in the courtroom, on the phone, and at every hearing.
