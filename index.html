Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I talk to police before hiring an attorney?
No. You have the right to remain silent, and you should use it. Anything you say to police can and will be used against you in court. Politely decline to answer questions and ask to speak with an attorney immediately. This is true even if you are innocent. Misstatements made under pressure can be used to damage your case later.
How much does a criminal defense attorney cost?
Fees vary based on the complexity of the charges, the stage of the case, and whether the matter goes to trial. Matthew offers flexible payment arrangements and will be upfront about fees during your free consultation. The cost of not having strong representation is almost always higher.
What happens at a preliminary hearing?
A preliminary hearing is one of the most important stages in a Pennsylvania criminal case. The judge decides whether the Commonwealth has enough evidence to proceed. Matthew has had countless cases dismissed or significantly reduced at this stage. It is an opportunity that should not be wasted with unprepared representation.
What is a motion to suppress?
A motion to suppress challenges whether evidence against you was obtained legally. If police violated your constitutional rights during a search or arrest, a judge may exclude that evidence from trial. In many cases, a successful suppression motion means the charges are dropped entirely because the Commonwealth has nothing left to prove their case.
Can Matthew help if I’ve already been charged?
Yes. Matthew handles cases at every stage, from the moment of arrest through trial and beyond. Even if charges have already been filed, there are often powerful options available to challenge the evidence, negotiate the charges, or prepare a winning defense at trial. The earlier you get representation the better, but it is never too late to call.
How quickly can Matthew take my case?
Matthew understands that criminal cases are urgent. He responds to inquiries quickly and can often schedule a same-day or next-day consultation. If you or a loved one is in custody, call directly and he will prioritize getting you the information you need right away.
